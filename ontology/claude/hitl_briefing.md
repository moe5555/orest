# "Human in the Loop" as an Artistic Subject: A Field Map for the BLOOM / Elektra Production

## TL;DR
- The richest, least-exhausted vein for your production is **the confirmation/authorisation moment staged as a live mechanic the audience or an on-stage operator must actually perform** (Ontroerend Goed's binding votes, Killbox's two-player asymmetry, Papers Please's stamp, Lauren Lee McCarthy's human-as-machine inversion) — *not* the lonely-drone-operator-with-PTSD portrait, which is now a cliché (Grounded as play and opera, Omer Fast's *5,000 Feet*) that critics increasingly argue humanises the operator in a way that lets the system off the hook.
- Your Greek frame points in an underused direction: Robert Icke's *Oresteia* already stages Agamemnon's sacrifice as a **timed deliberation under visible countdown clocks**, and Milo Rau's *Orestes in Mosul* turns the trilogy into a media-and-justice apparatus with live/recorded feeds — but **no existing work fuses the Oresteia (or Elektra) with a live machine-vision targeting system trained on the actual audience**, which is precisely the gap your production can occupy.
- For a German audience there is one precedent you must cite and differentiate from: Rimini Protokoll's *Der Zauberlehrling* (2009, Düsseldorfer Schauspielhaus / HAU Berlin) **put Stanislav Petrov himself on stage** — the officer who refused to trust the automated early-warning system. The nuclear-refusal lineage (Petrov, Arkhipov, *WarGames*) is the strongest pre-AI ancestor of your BLOOM operator, and Petrov is the exact *inverse* of automation bias.

## Key Findings
The field organises best by **dramaturgical strategy**, not by medium. Below are the moves artists have actually made, with what worked, what failed, and what critics said — clustered so you can pick a strategy rather than a checklist.

## Details

### STRATEGY 1 — Put the audience at the console (make the spectator authorise)
This is the most direct analogue to your human-in-the-loop confirmation dashboard, and the tradition where your production can be most original.

**Ontroerend Goed (Ghent) — the complicity works.** The Belgian collective has spent fifteen years building shows whose entire engine is forcing a real audience decision and then staging its consequences. In *Audience* (2011) the house camera is turned on the spectators, then the performers single out and verbally bully an audience member; the show's true subject becomes the audience's failure to intervene — complicity through passivity. In *Fight Night* (2013) each spectator holds an electronic clicker and eliminates five candidates round by round; the dramaturgical trick is that **the choices progressively narrow and the criteria are never disclosed**, so voters grasp what they have authorised only after it is irreversible ("it is only after the votes have been cast that we fully understand the implications of what we have chosen," per Stage and Cinema). *£¥€$ (Lies)* seats the audience at a casino table running the financial system. The lesson: their power comes from a **real, binding, escalating decision plus delayed revelation of its meaning** — the exact structure of your auto-confirming countdown. Their documented risk is that provocation tips into a manipulation the audience resents; several reviewers accuse *Audience* of crossing an ethical line with its targeting of a young woman.

**Rod Dickinson — The Milgram Re-enactment (2002, CCA Glasgow, with Graeme Edler and Steve Rushton).** A word-perfect, roughly two-to-three-hour restaging of Milgram's "Obedience to Authority" experiment, scripted from the Yale archive transcripts (conditions 02 and 08), inside an exact facsimile of the Yale Interaction Laboratory with reproduction 1960s shock equipment. Dickinson's insight, stated in interview, was that Milgram's experiment *already relied on theatre and dramaturgical structure* to produce its result — actors playing a scientist and a "learner." As an artwork it was deliberately anti-cathartic (the Guardian's reviewer called it one of the longest evenings of her life), which is itself the strategy: refusing the audience the release that would let them off the hook. It is the canonical "obedience-to-authority as performance" precedent behind your auto-confirm mechanic; Artur Żmijewski's *Repetition* (2005), restaging the Stanford Prison Experiment, is its companion piece.

**Why this lineage matters for your framing:** the two-man rule, the countdown, and the auto-confirm are all dramatisations of the Milgram finding. If you stage the confirmation step, you are already staging Milgram — and naming *that* lineage may be fresher than the drone-operator one.

### STRATEGY 2 — The human who behaves like, or stands in for, the machine (the inversion)
The user flagged this as most relevant, and it is genuinely underexploited.

**Lauren Lee McCarthy — LAUREN (2017), SOMEONE (2019), 24h HOST (2019).** In *LAUREN* the artist installs cameras, mics and networked locks/faucets in a stranger's home and personally becomes "a human Alexa," watching 24/7 and responding when the occupant calls out — a human performing the automated system to expose "the hidden role of human labor in an automated future." *SOMEONE* inverts the vantage: gallery visitors sit at a **command centre of surveillance monitors** and become the smart-home "intelligence" for four real households, choosing when to intervene. This is the precise inversion of human-in-the-loop — the human *is* the loop. McCarthy is also the creator of p5.js and co-director of the Processing Foundation, so she will resonate directly with your Creative Technologies team; she is the single most on-point artist for your inversion strand.

**The Mechanical Turk / ghost-work lineage.** *The Cleaners* (2018, dir. Hans Block & Moritz Riesewieck — German-produced, premiered Sundance) follows Manila content moderators making binary DELETE/IGNORE decisions under brutal quota. Per ProPublica's screening notes, "a typical 'cleaner' views 25,000 posts a day, deciding what to delete and what to ignore"; the film builds a chorus out of the click-rhythm "delete, delete, ignore, delete." It is the human-in-the-loop of platform moderation — traumatised, outsourced, invisible — and is directly stageable as your "six-dimensional scoring + confirm" analogue. Andrew Norman Wilson's *Workers Leaving the Googleplex* and Sarah T. Roberts's scholarship sit behind this.

### STRATEGY 3 — Stay inside one operator's voice (the monologue of the person in the loop)
This is the most CROWDED and now most CLICHÉD approach — flagged so you can avoid it.

**George Brant, Grounded (2013 play; 2023/24 opera).** The one-woman play — an F-16 pilot reassigned to fly a Reaper from a trailer near Las Vegas — is the canonical drone-operator monologue. The Tesori/Brant opera (Washington National Opera 2023; Metropolitan Opera opening night, 22 September 2024, with Emily D'Angelo as Jess, ~45 minutes cut for the Met) added a "Drone Squadron" chorus, a dissociated "Also Jess" double, and Mimi Lien's set of 224 floor and 84 ceiling LED panels. **Critical reception is your warning label:** the Washington Post wrote that "Mayer's literal widescreen treatment broadens the scope of 'Grounded' to the point where the target too often falls out of sight"; Zachary Woolfe in the NYT called the Met staging "bloodless" and "bland"; reviewers mocked an aria about building a PowerPoint slide. It is hard evidence that scaling the lonely-operator story with spectacular screens *dilutes* rather than sharpens the moral question.

**German-language currency:** Brant's play, as *Am Boden* (trans. Henning Bochert), has been staged repeatedly across the German-speaking region — Schauspiel Frankfurt (dir. Anselm Weber), Staatstheater Darmstadt (audience in silent-disco headphones, placed inside the pilot's head), Landestheater Tübingen, Wolfgang Borchert Theater Münster, Landestheater Coburg, Theater Biel-Solothurn (Swiss premiere) — so a German audience may well have already seen the material. That saturation is exactly why you should *not* centre a single-operator monologue.

**Omer Fast, *5,000 Feet is the Best* (2011)** and the wider "traumatised remote killer" screen genre confirm the point: the lonely operator with PTSD is exhausted. Your degradation-of-accuracy-over-the-evening device is smarter because it indicts the *system's epistemics* rather than the operator's feelings.

### STRATEGY 4 — Make the machine's language the text (found-text / documentary poetics)
**Solmaz Sharif, LOOK (2016, Graywolf).** Built from the US Department of Defense *Dictionary of Military and Associated Terms* (the 2007 edition, a 770-page PDF): military terms are set in small caps and dropped into intimate domestic scenes. The book's epigraph and title are themselves a DoD entry — "LOOK —— In mine warfare, a period during which a mine circuit is receptive of an influence" — which Sharif glosses aloud as "the period during which someone steps on a mine and dies." Her key line, "Daily I sit / with the language / they've made / of our language," is the poetic version of your projected threat-scores: euphemism as the interface layer that makes killing administrable. Directly usable as projected or spoken text. Aria Aber's *Hard Damage* (2019) is a companion collection.

**The view from beneath the drone.** Atef Abu Saif's diary *The Drone Eats with Me: A Gaza Diary* (2014/2016) and Mosab Abu Toha's poetry *Things You May Find Hidden in My Ear* (2022, American Book Award) and *Forest of Noise* (2024) supply the counter-position to the operator's console — the surveilled civilian who hears the drone constantly overhead ("the ever-present menace of surveillance drones policing a people unwelcome in their own land"). Including this voice is the single most effective way to avoid the "operator's-eye-view only" trap that critics attack in the drone-monologue genre.

**Kafka, *In the Penal Colony* — the machine that executes the sentence.** Philip Glass's chamber opera (premiered 31 August 2000, ACT Seattle, dir. JoAnne Akalaitis, libretto Rudy Wurlitzer) stages the Officer lovingly explaining an execution apparatus that inscribes the sentence into the condemned's flesh; the Visitor is the "human in the loop" who witnesses and, by declining to authorise, "fails" (Glass's own framing: "The Visitor gets away, but, by avoiding judgment, actually fails"). Reviewers note the machine is effectively the protagonist. A canonical bureaucratic-killing text with a ready, portable operatic model.

### STRATEGY 5 — Show the interface, put the viewer at the screen (visual art / installation)
**Lawrence Abu Hamdan — "forensic listening."** *Walled Unwalled* (2018, Tate Modern; MoMA 2023) and the *Air Conditioning* / *Air Pressure* works (Mudam Luxembourg, built from UN open-source airspace-violation data) turn the viewer into an "ear-witness," reconstructing state violence from acoustic evidence. His mode — the artist-investigator delivering an audiovisual essay from behind glass — is a strong model for an *operator-lecture register that indicts the apparatus* rather than romanticising the operator. Forensic Architecture (with whom he has worked) sits alongside.

**Hito Steyerl — *How Not to Be Seen: A Fucking Didactic Educational .MOV File* (2013).** A mock-instructional video on evading computer vision, staging the resolution target and the pixel as the units of machine visibility. Adjacent to the operational-image canon you already hold, but specifically relevant to your **deliberate degradation of accuracy** device.

**Joseph DeLappe & Biome Collective (Malath Abbas, Tom deMajo, Albert Elwin) — Killbox (2016).** A game, but installation-native (custom two-player kiosk; BAFTA Scotland "Best Computer Game" nominee 2016). One player wanders an abstract ground-space modelled on "a farm modeled on one where a grandmother and her family were killed by a drone strike in Pakistan's north Waziristan area in 2012"; the other looks down through a drone feed and fires. The **asymmetry is the mechanic** — the two players never share a screen or a moral frame, dramatising what Kill Screen called the "yawning expanse" between the person confirming and the person targeted. Designer Malath Abbas: "At IndieCade we had a number of players cry once they realized the consequences of the in-game actions." This two-position split is a highly transferable staging idea.

### STRATEGY 6 — Make the player the operator (games)
**Papers, Please (Lucas Pope, 2013).** The player is a border inspector stamping APPROVED/DENIED under time pressure and shifting rules, paid per processed person, forced to trade family survival against mercy. The mechanic *is* the argument: bureaucratic complicity through an escalating paperwork loop. The critical split is useful to you — Patricia Hernandez argued it ultimately becomes "a fantasy of white-collar empowerment" in which the gamey heroism outweighs the exercised empathy. Pre-empt that in your own dashboard design: make sure the operator role feels like dread and drudgery, not empowerment.

**Related implicating mechanics:** *This War of Mine* (civilian survival), *Spec Ops: The Line* (the white-phosphorus sequence that retroactively implicates the player), *Orwell* and *Beholder* (the surveillance-state clerk), and Molleindustria's *Unmanned* and *Every Day the Same Dream*. The common thread: the work withholds the "you are complicit" reveal until *after* the player has acted.

### STRATEGY 7 — The Greek frame for automated/bureaucratic killing (your home turf)
**Robert Icke, Oresteia (Almeida 2015; Park Avenue Armory 2022).** The most directly relevant precedent. Icke restages Agamemnon's sacrifice of Iphigenia as an on-stage, present-tense **deliberation under a visible countdown**, with LED timers and a persistent ticking marking each character's time of death (the display also announces each death's exact time as it happens, and even times the intervals). The whole production is built as a decision running against a clock — extremely close to your auto-confirm timer. Study how Icke makes the *deliberation itself* the drama rather than the act; note also that reviewers found the translucent-screen-plus-timer design "overfamiliar," a caution about the aesthetic becoming a cliché.

**Milo Rau / NTGent, Orestes in Mosul (2019).** The trilogy staged partly in ISIS-devastated Mosul with a mixed European/Iraqi cast, integrating video shot on location; Rau reads the Oresteia as the birth of a justice system that replaces the revenge loop. The model for using the Oresteia as a *media-and-justice apparatus* with live/recorded feeds — and the source of a live scholarly debate (see *Canadian Theatre Review* on "empathy, anagnorisis and the politics of recognition") over whether such intercultural staging ethically succeeds or merely aestheticises others' suffering.

**Bryan Doerries / Theater of War (since 2008).** Staged readings of Sophocles' *Ajax* and *Philoctetes* for military audiences (350-plus performances, from the Pentagon to Guantánamo), each followed by a town-hall discussion. The dramaturgy is the *post-show authorisation of speech* — the audience, not the actors, completes the work ("not a performance… a catalyst"). A model for treating a Greek text as catalyst rather than spectacle and for building veteran/operator testimony into the evening.

**Annie Dorsen — algorithmic theatre, incl. Prometheus Firebringer (2023).** Not about killing, but methodologically vital: Dorsen builds pieces where custom algorithms and chatbots generate the performance live, and she argues sharply that AI cannot make art — the danger is "human-created art that looks like it was generated by a machine." *Prometheus Firebringer* reworks the lost third Aeschylus Prometheus play with live generative text and on-screen source citations. Her work (shown at HAU Berlin and steirischer herbst) is the reference for *how to put a live algorithm on stage without mystifying it*.

### STRATEGY 8 — The nuclear / missile-crew lineage (the pre-AI human in the loop)
This is the strongest historical spine for your production, and for a German audience it has a specific local precedent.

**Rimini Protokoll, Der Zauberlehrling (premiere 22 May 2009, CENTRAL / Düsseldorfer Schauspielhaus; HAU Berlin 3 June 2009; concept Helgard Haug & Daniel Wetzel).** Documentary theatre that **put the real Stanislav Petrov on stage** (alongside experts Günter Klepke, Markus Kompa and Herdís Sigurgrímsdóttir) — the Soviet officer who on 26 September 1983 declined to authorise a nuclear counter-strike when the automated satellite early-warning system falsely reported incoming US missiles. Nachtkritik explicitly framed his refusal through Melville: "Wie Melvilles berühmter Schreiber Bartleby sagte auch er: 'I would prefer not to.'" This is the human-decision-versus-automated-warning theme staged, in Germany, with the actual man — you must reference and differentiate from it.

**Petrov as a recurring artistic subject.** *The Man Who Saved the World* (2014 docudrama) dramatised Petrov; small-scale Petrov operas exist (e.g. a ~15-minute "Petrov: A Cold War Opera," workshopped 2025 — verify status before citing as a major precedent). Vasili Arkhipov, the 1962 submarine officer who withheld consent to a nuclear torpedo, is the companion "man who said no." The artistic interest for you is how each work handles **the moment of refusal** — Petrov's is a refusal to trust the machine's determination, i.e. the anti-automation-bias hero, the exact inverse of your auto-confirming operator.

**Film canon (pre-AI):** *Dr. Strangelove*, *Fail-Safe*, *WarGames* (the WOPR and the human refusal to let the machine launch), *The Day After*, *Threads*, *By Dawn's Early Light*. Each dramatises permissive action links, the two-man rule, or dead-hand automation; *WarGames* in particular is the ur-text of "the machine will launch unless a human intervenes."

### STRATEGY 9 — The bureaucratic / administrative loop (the clerk, the signature)
**Hannah Arendt / Eichmann afterlives.** Arendt's "banality of evil" is the conceptual root of the moral-crumple-zone idea (Madeleine Clare Elish), which the user already holds. Artistic afterlives include *The Eichmann Show* (2015) and, most importantly for you, **Peter Weiss's *Die Ermittlung* (The Investigation, 1965)** — a documentary "oratorio" built verbatim from the Frankfurt Auschwitz trial transcripts, the German-language model par excellence for turning an administrative record into stage text. Melville's *Bartleby* ("I would prefer not to") is the literary archetype of refusal within the clerical loop — and, per the Petrov citation above, already the tacit key to the refusal you would be staging.

### German-language digital-theatre context to cite (for a German audience and funders)
- **Rimini Protokoll, Uncanny Valley / Unheimliches Tal (2018, Münchner Kammerspiele; Berliner Festspiele 2020):** an animatronic double of author Thomas Melle delivers the lecture alone — machine "stability" versus human instability, and the pointed question of "who is speaking, and what is its programme."
- **Rimini Protokoll, Remote X (since 2013, HAU Berlin):** ~50 spectators led through the real city by a synthetic navigation voice — a self-experiment in obedience to an algorithm and in swarm/predictability, highly relevant to authority-to-algorithm dramaturgy. (And *Situation Rooms*, 2013, which the user already has — note its drone-strike and surveillance-monitor rooms.)
- **Kris Verdonck / A Two Dogs Company:** *ACTOR #1* (2010, premiered Theater der Welt), *ENTITIES*, *BRASS*, *DARK* — machines and autonomous sculptures as performers, theatre "without people," the human/machine agency boundary; scholarly volume *Machine Made Silence*.
- **CyberRäuber (Marcel Karnapke & Björn Lengers, since 2016):** VR/AI theatre at Schauspiel Dortmund, Deutsches Theater Berlin, Badisches Staatstheater Karlsruhe, Landestheater Linz.
- **Staatstheater Augsburg Digitaltheater:** *Unser Leben in den Wäldern* (a woman hiding from "a world full of drones and state surveillance," full-CGI stage) and a *Menschenfeind* with a ChatGPT-4 "Molusk" as a Lehrstück on the limits of AI.
- **Akademie für Theater und Digitalität, Dortmund:** projects reading **live biometric audience data** during performance (*Excentric*, with the Dortmunder Philharmoniker) and mixed-reality "surveillance, interaction and cooperation" webs (*Department of Interfaced Dimensions*) — the closest institutional practice anywhere to your apparatus, and a natural collaboration/funding partner.
- **Angela Richter, Supernerds (2015, Schauspiel Köln / WDR):** a surveillance evening that live-taps spectators' phone cameras and streams Julian Assange in from the Ecuadorian embassy.
- **Cie Nicole Seiler, "Human in the loop" (Lausanne; touring 2024–25, e.g. tanzhaus nrw):** a dance piece in which an AI generates fresh choreographic instructions before each show — the *only* stage work actually using your working title, so check your title against it.
- **Scholarship:** Martina Leeker (ed. *Performing the Digital*, transcript 2016; *Maschinen, Medien, Performances*, 2001; essays on "posthumanes Theater"); Benjamin Wihstutz (*Neue Methoden der Theaterwissenschaft*, transcript, which discusses Deutsches Theater Göttingen's drive-through staging of Juli Zeh's surveillance-dystopia *Corpus Delicti*); the transcript "Theater" series and Theater der Zeit as the key venues; the open-access *Netztheater* (2020). Thread through the theory the user already holds — Elke Schwarz, Lucy Suchman, M.L. Cummings on automation bias, Elish's moral crumple zone.

## Recommendations

**Stage now (highest-value, least-clichéd moves):**
1. **Build the evening around a binding audience/operator confirmation with delayed revelation** (Ontroerend Goed + Papers Please + Milgram), not around an operator's inner life. Your auto-confirm countdown already is this; make the *criteria* opaque and reveal consequences only after the vote, as *Fight Night* does.
2. **Use the inversion at least once** (McCarthy, *The Cleaners*): make a human visibly *be* the scoring system — the six-dimensional threat score voiced or performed live by a person — so the "machine" is unmasked as labour and prejudice.
3. **Let the machine's language be the text** (Sharif's *LOOK* method) and **include the view from beneath** (Abu Toha, Abu Saif) so the console is never the only vantage. This is the specific antidote to the critique levelled at *Grounded* and Fast.
4. **Explicitly invoke and differentiate from *Der Zauberlehrling* / Petrov.** For a German audience this is the honest and legible move, and it positions your BLOOM operator as the automation-bias *inverse* of Petrov's Bartleby-style refusal.
5. **Mine Icke's *Oresteia* countdown structure** for how to make deliberation-against-a-clock dramatic, and Rau's *Orestes in Mosul* and Dorsen's algorithmic theatre for how to integrate live feeds/algorithms without mystifying them.

**Avoid or handle with care:** the lonely drone operator with PTSD (*Grounded*, Fast — exhausted, and critiqued as system-exculpating); spectacular LED literalism that makes "the target fall out of sight" (the *Grounded* opera's failure mode); the "overfamiliar" translucent-screen-plus-ticking-timer look (already flagged in Icke reviews); and any confirm-step that feels empowering or fun (the *Papers Please* "white-collar empowerment" critique).

**Benchmarks that would change the plan:** if audience testing shows the confirmation feels like a game the audience *enjoys*, redesign toward drudgery and dread (*Papers Please*, Milgram). If the degradation-of-accuracy device reads as a mere technical glitch rather than an epistemic indictment, foreground it with Sharif-style euphemism text and an on-stage human voicing the "corrected" scores. If reviewers or dramaturgs read the piece as humanising the operator, add more of the view-from-beneath (Strategy 4) until the balance tips back to the system.

## Caveats
- Several German productions are documented via venue/press pages and, for the Petrov/*Zauberlehrling* staging, the artists' own site plus Nachtkritik and Wikipedia; confirm live details with the theatres.
- No production literally titled "Human in the Loop" exists in German Stadttheater drama; only Cie Nicole Seiler's dance piece "Human in the loop" (touring 2024–25, AI-generated choreography) uses the phrase — the title is available but check it against that work and the German term "Mensch in der Schleife" already common in the Bundeswehr/UN autonomous-weapons debate.
- I could not verify a current German-theatre Milgram staging or a steirischer herbst autonomous-weapons production; treat both as gaps worth a direct archive check.
- The Petrov opera material is small-scale/workshop; verify status before citing as a major precedent.
- Drone-strike casualty and approval figures cited around *Killbox* (US government's claim of 64–116 civilian deaths Jan 2009–end 2015 versus the Bureau of Investigative Journalism's estimate of 380–801; ~58% US public approval) come from journalism around the game, not primary audit — use as indicative, not definitive.

### What appears UNDERDONE — gaps your production can occupy
- **No one has fused a live machine-vision targeting apparatus with Greek tragedy.** Icke uses a countdown; Rau uses video; neither runs a real-time scoring/targeting system on the actual audience in the room. This is genuinely open ground.
- **The auto-confirming timer as the tragic mechanism** — the confirmation that happens *by default if the human does nothing* — is a fresh dramatisation of automation bias that the existing operator-monologue works never stage, precisely because they keep the fatal decision human and deliberate.
- **Deliberate, visible degradation of the system's accuracy over the evening** as a dramaturgical arc is, as far as this research found, unattempted: most works present the machine as either reliable or catastrophically wrong once, not as *progressively less trustworthy while still being obeyed* — which is the sharpest possible indictment of the "human in the loop" as decorative safeguard.
- **The chorus as the surveilled population** (Loraux's "mourning voice" plus Abu Toha's view-from-beneath) fused with the operator's console would give you the two-position asymmetry of *Killbox* inside a tragic-chorus structure — a combination no existing work occupies, and the strongest way to make Elektra/the Oresteia carry the human-in-the-loop argument rather than merely decorate it.
