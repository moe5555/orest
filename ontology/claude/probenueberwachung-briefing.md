# Surveilling the Rehearsal Process
## Research briefing: recording the Probenprozess, Palantir-style analysis, and staging the result

---

## 0. The actual problem

You will generate somewhere between 100 and 400 hours of video and audio, plus derived data (transcripts, scores, dossiers, embeddings). You have roughly 90–120 minutes on stage. The compression ratio is between 100:1 and 250:1.

Every decision in this briefing follows from that ratio. The interesting question is not "what do we record" — record everything, storage is cheap — but **what is the selection principle, and is the selection principle itself visible to the audience?**

This is the single most important formal question in the piece, because it is also the political question. Lavender, HessenData and Gotham are selection principles. A targeting system is a compression algorithm with consequences. If your dramaturgy of compression is invisible or arbitrary, you have made a montage. If it is visible and motivated, you have made the argument.

**Three families of selection principle, with very different politics:**

| Principle | Who selects | What the audience learns |
|---|---|---|
| **Curatorial** — the makers pick the good bits | You | Nothing about systems. This is a normal edit. |
| **Algorithmic** — the system's own scores drive selection (show every moment scored ≥7 Gefahr) | BLOOM | The system's blind spots become the evening's structure. Strongest option. |
| **Exhaustive/formal** — a rule with no judgement (every Tuesday 14:00–14:05; every frame in which nobody is present) | The rule | Duration and banality as content. Cold, rigorous, risky. |

The strongest version is probably algorithmic selection with the rule *stated out loud*, so the audience knows they are watching the system's taste and not yours.

## 1. Precedent: long surveillance of one place, compressed into one work

### Michael Klier, *Der Riese* (1983, 82 min, ZDF/Klier)
Assembled entirely from existing surveillance footage — airports, department stores, banks, subway underpasses, private grounds — with no images shot by the director. Farocki's introduction (NBK Berlin, 2014) notes Klier drove around for months recording surveillance feeds and copying archival material. The soundtrack is Mahler and Wagner, and the combination produces what Media Art Net's Reinhard Wolf described as the impression of a central apparatus as an anonymous, omnipresent subject that sees everything and is itself invisible.

**Why it matters to you:** Klier solved your banality problem by *fictionalising the apparatus as a character* — the giant: strong, not intelligent, clumsily zooming, trying to frame a sailboat or a pair of lovers. Artforum (1985) argued that this fictionalisation is what lifts the film above scaremongering documentary, because it reveals the mentality behind surveillance rather than merely its extent. Farocki's phrase for the montage effect is that Klier puts the images in the subjunctive: *could these be images of a film narrative?* — "a productive misconstruction."

That is exactly your Whisper-hallucination material. The mishearing is the subjunctive.

- Farocki's text: https://www.ercatx.org/michael-klier-the-giant-1983-by-harun-farocki/
- Longer essay: https://bordercrossingsmag.com/article/surveillance-fantasies-on-michael-kliers-der-riese
- Note: hard to source. MoMA holds it. Try Arsenal Berlin / NBK, or contact Klier directly.

### Jill Magid, *Evidence Locker* (2004, Liverpool Biennial / FACT / Tate Liverpool)
Magid spent 31 days in Liverpool working with Citywatch (Merseyside Police + City Council), then operating 242 CCTV cameras. Footage is deleted after 31 days unless preserved as evidence — but a Subject Access Request forces the police to pull and store footage of you for seven years. Magid filed 31 of them, **writing each one as a love letter / diary entry to the operators**. She wore a red trench coat, phoned the control room with her location, and had the police film her — in *Trust* (18 min) she closed her eyes and let the operators guide her through the city by voice. The resulting locker holds 35+ entries and over 11 hours of footage. She credited the police as crew and director.

**Two transferable moves:**
1. **The bureaucratic form as the poetic form.** The access request is the text. Your equivalent: BLOOM's SITREP template filled in with grief.
2. **The operators as characters with names.** She built a relationship with the people watching. Your operator is a member of the ensemble.

- https://www.jillmagid.com/projects/evidence-locker-2
- https://www.biennial.com/artists/jill-magid/

### Manu Luksch, *Faceless* (2007)
Feature made entirely from UK CCTV footage obtained by subject access request. Because the law requires other people's faces to be obscured, everyone except the protagonist is blanked — the legal compliance procedure becomes the film's science-fiction premise (a world of faceless people). **The redaction is the aesthetic.** Directly applicable: if you must anonymise anyone in your material for legal reasons, make the anonymisation visible and diegetic rather than tidy.

### Others worth a look, briefly
- **Hasan Elahi, *Tracking Transience*** — after an FBI investigation, self-surveilled obsessively; tens of thousands of images of meals, toilets, airports. The banality is the defence. Overwhelming quantity as a strategy for making an audience *feel* dataset size.
- **Sophie Calle, *La Filature* (1981)** — hired a private detective to follow her; the artwork is his report next to her diary of the same day. **Two accounts of the same hours, side by side.** This is the single cleanest structure for your banality/significance axis, and it is trivially adaptable: BLOOM's dossier on a rehearsal day, projected beside a performer's own account of that day.
- **Nicholas Felton, *Feltron Annual Reports* (2005–2014)** — a year of personal data as an infographic annual report. The aesthetics of turning a life into a corporate deliverable. Look particularly at the 2010/2011 reports, where he logged conversations.
- **Dries Depoorter** — contemporary Belgian artist working with public CCTV, AI and social media (*The Follower*, *Jaywalking*, *Surveillance Speaker*). Closest living practice to yours; worth checking what he's done recently so you don't repeat it.
- **Kyle McDonald, *People Staring at Computers* (2011)** — installed capture software in Apple Stores, collected faces; the Secret Service seized his equipment. Useful as a cautionary tale about consent, and as evidence that the *unremarkable face of someone concentrating* is a genuinely arresting image.

## 2. The rehearsal process as material

Your instinct that "Probenprozess ist etwas, das Leute gerne sehen" is correct, and there is a tradition behind it.

**Nature Theater of Oklahoma, *No Dice* (2007) and the *Life and Times* cycle (2009–)** are the most rigorous verbatim-from-recording works. *Life and Times* began with the instruction "tell me your life story" to ensemble member Kristin Worrall, recorded over 16+ hours of telephone calls, then transcribed **verbatim including every "um," "ahh," pause and digression**, and set to music as libretto. Episodes 1–4 won an Obie; the full run at Norwich was a 12-hour event. In *No Dice*, recorded phone conversations were fed to performers through earpieces during the show.

Three things they prove:
1. Ordinary recorded speech, kept fully intact including disfluency, becomes strange and gripping *if the formal frame is severe enough* (music, choreography, earpieces). Left unframed it is just tape.
2. Length is a legitimate dramaturgical tool but requires the audience to be told what they're in for.
3. The "um" is the point. Your Whisper transcripts, hallucinations included, are the equivalent — but they need a hard formal container.

**Also relevant, briefly:**
- **Milo Rau / NTGent** — the making-of film as a component of the work; the Ghent Manifesto's rules about process transparency. If your surveillance material is *the* making-of, you're inside a form he has already institutionalised.
- **Christoph Schlingensief** — continuous self-documentation, the director present and audible, the apparatus never hidden. German audiences will read your visible operator through him.
- **Gob Squad** — live camera crossing the backstage/onstage boundary; performers watching themselves on monitors. The technique is well-worn in Germany, which is an argument for using *recorded* rather than *live* feed as your primary material.
- **Forced Entertainment / Tim Etchells** — writing on duration, failure and the unedited. Etchells on what happens when a performance refuses to select is directly useful theory.
- **Katie Mitchell's live-cinema method** — the *making* of the image is the show. Inverse of what you want (you want the *reading* of the image to be the show), but the audience-attention mechanics are well documented.

**The specifically German trap here:** open rehearsals, Probenbesuche and making-of documentation are institutionally normal in the German system. If your material reads as "backstage access," you get charm and lose the knife. The surveillance frame has to be *cold* — an apparatus that does not know it is looking at art.

## 3. The two frames a German audience will supply for free

You do not control what the audience brings. In Germany they will bring two things, both of which you should use deliberately rather than let leak in.

### 3.1 Die Akte — the Stasi file
An operative dossier on a small group of people in a room, accumulated over weeks, full of banal detail and confident wrong inferences, is *the* Stasi file form. Operative Personenkontrolle, IM-Berichte, the tone of institutional certainty applied to nothing.

- The dominant popular reference is **Das Leben der Anderen (2006)**, and the scholarly critique of it — that it sentimentalises the listener into a redeemable figure — is the same critique now levelled at drone-operator drama. Know it so you don't repeat it.
- **Cornelia Schleime, *Bis auf weitere gute Zusammenarbeit* (1993)** — the painter obtained her own Stasi file and made a series of works pairing the observers' reports with staged photographs of herself. Formally: the file text beside the image it claims to describe. Same structure as Calle's *La Filature*, in a German register.
- The **Aktenberg** — thousands of sacks of hand-shredded files still being reconstructed — is a ready-made image of an archive that resists compression.

### 3.2 Palantir in Germany — live, ongoing, in the newspaper
This is the more useful frame because it is present tense.

- **BVerfG, 16 February 2023** (1 BvR 1547/19, 1 BvR 2634/20), on complaints brought by the GFF: automated data analysis by police in Hesse (HessenData, running Palantir Gotham) and the Hamburg provision were held partly unconstitutional — a serious interference with informational self-determination, with legal bases that failed to limit the intensity of the interference. Specific criticisms included that it was unclear which data categories could be analysed and whether the system distinguished suspects from, e.g., people recorded only as tipsters.
- **23 July 2025:** GFF filed a further Verfassungsbeschwerde, with eight complainants including a defence lawyer, activists and members of a football fan-support group, against Art. 61a BayPAG, which permits "Data Mining" in Bavaria via a Gotham-based system (VeRA). GFF's framing: Bavarian police surveil masses of people who have never been suspected of anything, without transparency, control mechanisms or protection against discrimination. The CCC supported the complaint and criticised Bavarian police for making themselves dependent for years on deliberately opaque software. https://freiheitsrechte.org/themen/freiheit-im-digitalen-zeitalter/palantir-bayern
- **Deployment:** Hesse, Bavaria and NRW use restricted variants; a fourth Land has since been reported. The federal level was contested through 2025 (Dobrindt examining a Bund-level deployment) and, per Campact in February 2026, the Justice Ministry declined Palantir for federal authorities.
- **The Hesse history is theatre-ready:** the 2017 procurement, a parliamentary Untersuchungsausschuss, procurement-law violations, and a retroactive statutory amendment that civil-liberties campaigners dubbed the "Lex Palantir."
- **The sovereignty angle:** Palantir staff must work inside German police authorities to maintain the system; Palantir is subject to the US CLOUD Act; German alternatives existed and were never commissioned (NDR *Strg_F*, 2025). The Fraunhofer SIT source-code audit found no hidden backdoors, which is its own kind of reassurance.

**Dramaturgical use:** a five-minute segment in which BLOOM's terms of service, data-processing agreement and support arrangements are read aloud in bureaucratic German is more chilling than any drone footage, and it is *funny*, which buys you the tonal range you need.

## 4. What Palantir-style analysis actually looks like (so you can build the stage language)

Don't invent this. The forms are specific and reproducible.

### The object/ontology model
Gotham's core move is not analysis, it is **ontology**: the world is decomposed into Objects (person, vehicle, event, location, document), Properties, and Links. Everything downstream depends on this schema. **Entity resolution** merges records from different sources into one "person object" — and merges wrongly, which is where the drama lives.

*Stage translation:* the schema itself, projected, edited live. Your existing plan to change the classification schema mid-week without announcement is the strongest single idea in your concept, because it stages the ontology as an authored, arbitrary artefact. Push it further: let the audience see a person object being split, or two people being merged.

### The link chart
Genealogy: Anacapa Sciences charts (1970s law enforcement training), IBM i2 Analyst's Notebook, then Gotham's graph view. Nodes and edges, force-directed layout, thickness by frequency.

*Stage translation:* the link chart is visually identical to the "crazy wall" of conspiracy cinema — corkboard, photos, red string. That resemblance is not a problem, it's the material. A link chart of an ensemble is a sociogram, and a sociogram of a rehearsal room is genuinely revealing and genuinely creepy: who talks to whom, who is peripheral, who is a broker. Your Netzwerkrelevanz dimension already computes this. **Show the graph accumulating across weeks in 30 seconds.**

### Pattern of life / activity-based intelligence (ABI)
Doctrine from US SIGINT/GEOINT: rather than identifying a known individual, construct a *normal pattern* from movement and association data and flag deviation. This is what licenses "signature strikes" — killing on the basis of behaviour rather than identity. The critique (Gregory, Weizman, Schwarz, Suchman) is that the pattern is constructed by the observer and the deviation is defined by the model.

*Stage translation:* this is the concept that makes rehearsal surveillance *mean* something. A rehearsal is definitionally a place where people do abnormal things repeatedly. A pattern-of-life model trained on a rehearsal room will classify the work itself as anomalous. **That is your central joke and your central argument, and I don't think anyone has staged it.**

### Confidence language
US IC standards (ICD 203; the Sherman Kent "Words of Estimative Probability" lineage) prescribe the vocabulary: *we assess with high/moderate/low confidence*, *likely*, *almost certainly*, and the explicit separation of confidence-in-sourcing from probability-of-event. German equivalent registers exist in BKA/Verfassungsschutz Lagebild language.

*Stage translation:* this is your text. It is already rhythmic, already euphemistic, already performable. A chorus speaking in estimative probability against a Sophocles chorus is a real formal idea.

### The intelligence product
Learn the genres and use them as scene forms: **SITREP** (situation report), **pattern-of-life dossier**, **target package**, **BDA** (battle damage assessment, i.e. what did we destroy and was it what we meant), **after-action report**. Each has a fixed structure. Each is a scene template.

### Artists who have already recreated intelligence interfaces
Kate Crawford & Vladan Joler (*Anatomy of an AI System*, *Calculating Empires*) for the wall-sized diagram as an aesthetic; Metahaven for the graphic language of the state; Sam Lavigne and Mimi Onuoha for critical database art; Forensic Architecture for the method of reconstructing an event from fragments and *showing the reconstruction*. Simon Denny you already have.

## 5. Banality ↔ significance ↔ creepiness: the mechanics

This oscillation is your stated interest, and it has a name in several disciplines.

**Apophenia** — the perception of pattern in random data. Your system is an apophenia machine: it never abstains, it scores people it cannot see, it generates full scene descriptions from a ceiling shot and silence. That is not a bug to hide, it is the thesis. **The system's confident output about nothing is the most valuable material you have.**

**The three canonical films of this exact problem, and what each teaches:**
- **Coppola, *The Conversation* (1974)** — Harry Caul re-listens to a recorded sentence until the emphasis shifts and the meaning inverts. He is right about the words and catastrophically wrong about the meaning. **This is your Whisper-hallucination scene, already written.** The technique: repetition with tiny variation, until the audience hears differently. Cheap to stage, devastating.
- **Haneke, *Caché* (2005)** — surveillance tapes with no identifiable sender; the horror is authorlessness. The long static take of a façade in which nothing happens. Teaches that unattributed observation is scarier than a named observer.
- **Antonioni, *Blow-Up* (1966)** — enlarge until the image dissolves into grain. Weizman's "threshold of detectability": at high enough magnification the evidence disappears into noise. Directly relevant to your planned degradation.

**On boredom and duration:** Lehmann's *Postdramatisches Theater* on non-narrative time; Warhol's *Empire* and *Sleep*; Marclay's *The Clock* (24h, and the lesson that exhaustive material becomes gripping only when a *single rigid rule* organises it). Norwegian slow TV and the Big Brother edit are the popular versions — Big Brother in particular is worth studying for the diary room (the subject narrating themselves to the apparatus) and for how the edit manufactures character from ambient footage.

**On the eerie:** Mark Fisher, *The Weird and the Eerie* — the eerie as a failure of absence (something where there should be nothing) or a failure of presence (nothing where there should be something). An empty rehearsal room recorded at 03:00, with BLOOM still scoring it, is a textbook eerie image and probably your best atmospheric asset. **Record the empty room. Every night.**

**On the paranoid reading:** Luc Boltanski, *Mysteries and Conspiracies* (2012) — the detective novel and the spy novel emerge alongside the modern state's project of investigating its own population. Good theory ballast for the dramaturgy.

**Practical tonal architecture.** The oscillation only works if all three registers are present and *deliberately sequenced*. A working pattern:

1. **Banal, unframed** — raw footage, real time, nothing happens. (60–90 seconds is a lot.)
2. **The same footage, scored** — overlay appears, scores appear, an assessment is spoken. The audience feels the material *acquire* meaning.
3. **The assessment turns out to be wrong** — or is revised, or contradicts a second source.
4. **The same footage again, now unscored** — and the audience cannot un-see the scores.

Step 4 is the whole piece. That is what surveillance does to a room.

## 6. CV and audio on recorded material: what's actually productive

Distinguish **techniques that produce a legible stage image at 15 metres** from techniques that only work on a laptop.

### Reads from the back row
- **Pose skeletons** (MediaPipe Pose, YOLO-pose, RTMPose). High contrast, instantly legible, and the *failures* are legible too — the skeleton on an empty chair, the skeleton on a coat rack. Extremely strong when superimposed on live performers doing the same movement.
- **Accumulated trajectory maps / movement heatmaps.** Weeks of tracked positions in the rehearsal room drawn as a single image. This is the picture of "your process" that nobody has ever seen. Aesthetically beautiful, conceptually exact (it *is* pattern-of-life), and cheap: centroid of each detected person per frame, accumulate, blur, colour-map.
- **Background subtraction ghosts.** MOG2 or a temporal median. Produces literal ghosts. Pair with an empty room.
- **Optical flow / motion energy images.** Farnebäck or RAFT; sum motion over an hour into one frame. A day of rehearsal as a single smear.
- **Slit-scan and time-lapse.** One vertical pixel-column per frame across a week → a single image of the week's rhythm. Bars of light for who was in the room when.
- **Bounding boxes with confidence numbers.** Banal, universally recognised, and the recognisability is the point. Keep the ugly default rendering; do not design it prettily.
- **Re-identification across cameras and days** (OSNet / TorchReID). The dramatically productive part is the **ID switch** — person 3 becomes person 7 becomes person 3. Identity as an unstable database field.

### Only works on close screens (use as printed matter or as an operator's private view)
- t-SNE / UMAP embedding maps of faces or frames. Gorgeous, illegible at distance. Print them as A0 posters in the foyer instead.
- Detailed link charts with labels.
- Full transcripts scrolling.

### Audio
- **Speaker diarisation** (pyannote.audio) — who spoke when, over eight weeks, as a single visual score. Instantly shows hierarchy: who talks, who doesn't, and who talked less each week.
- **Laughter and overlap detection.** Laughter is the thing your system already mis-scores. Build on that.
- **Speech-rate and prosody**, keyword spotting for a chosen lexicon (names, the word "target," the word "Vater").
- **The corpus of Whisper hallucinations as a found text.** ZDF subtitle credits, "Vielen Dank," phantom sentences in silence. Collect them all. Speak them. This is genuinely original material and nobody else has it.
- **Text-to-speech as the system's voice.** Slightly-wrong prosody reads as sinister with almost no effort. Two useful register options: a German TTS with subtly incorrect stress, or a *human* performer reading system output flatly — often more disturbing, and it puts a human in the loop of the machine's speech, which is thematically exact.

### One technical note
Given your ring-buffer architecture: log the **non-detections and dropped windows as explicit events** with timestamps. A visualisation of the system's blindness over eight weeks — every second it was not looking — is a better image than any detection, and it is the thing no real vendor would ever build.

## 7. Staging: making an audience feel the size of a dataset

Four proven strategies, roughly in order of cost:

**1. Exhaustive enumeration.** Read or display *everything* in one narrow category. Every time a specific person's name was said in eight weeks. Every occurrence of the word "ja." The comedy and the dread both come from the refusal to select. (Cf. Nature Theater of Oklahoma; cf. Kenneth Goldsmith's conceptual writing.)

**2. Physical mass.** Print it. A stack of paper of the full transcript, physically present, is a better dataset visualisation than any screen. Weight, spillage, the sound of it. Print the dossier on every ensemble member and hand it to them on stage.

**3. Audible/visible speed-up.** Play eight weeks at 5000× — the whole process as a 90-second shriek. Then play 90 seconds at 1×. The juxtaposition does the work.

**4. Spatial audio.** This is where your immersive ambition should concentrate, because video-projection theatre is exhausted in Germany and spatial audio is not.
- **Binaural over headphones** is the strongest available tool for making recorded material feel present: Simon McBurney's *The Encounter* is the reference work (whole audience in headphones, binaural head microphone on stage, recorded and live sound indistinguishable). The moment where the audience cannot tell whether a sound is live or recorded is *the human-in-the-loop problem as a perceptual experience* — the audience cannot verify what the system tells them. That is worth building the evening around.
- Janet Cardiff's audio walks for the technique of a recorded voice narrating the space you are in.
- Multichannel/ambisonic: ZKM's Klangdom and the TU Berlin wave-field-synthesis lineage if you have institutional access. Otherwise 8-channel around the auditorium is enough to place voices behind people.
- **The key effect:** put recorded rehearsal-room audio in the auditorium behind the audience. The audience becomes the rehearsal room. Cheap, and it does the "creepy" register better than any image.

**5. Performers against recordings of themselves.** A performer lip-syncing their own recorded rehearsal speech, including disfluency; or working *against* a recording of themselves from week two while the audience hears both. Low tech, high yield, and it stages the gap between the person and their data double.

## 8. Ethics and law: surveilling your own ensemble

Treat this as dramaturgy, not compliance. The consent process is itself material.

### The hard legal points (Germany)
- **Mitbestimmung.** Introducing video surveillance at a workplace triggers co-determination under **§ 87 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 BetrVG** (or the Personalvertretungsgesetz at a public Stadttheater). The right attaches to any technical installation *objectively capable* of monitoring behaviour or performance, regardless of whether monitoring is intended. Surveillance installed without works-council consent is unlawful even where there is a legitimate reason. A **Betriebsvereinbarung** is the recommended instrument. If your production is at an institution with a Betriebsrat/Personalrat, this is a real gate — start early.
- **Rechtsgrundlage.** § 26 BDSG (Beschäftigtendatenschutz) plus Art. 6 DSGVO; § 4 BDSG for publicly accessible spaces. Note that § 26 BDSG's status has been under pressure since the CJEU's 2023 Hessen ruling — take current legal advice rather than relying on any summary.
- **DSFA.** Systematic employee video monitoring requires a Datenschutz-Folgenabschätzung under Art. 35 DSGVO; German supervisory authorities list it explicitly.
- **AI Act Art. 5(1)(f):** emotion recognition in the workplace is prohibited, with no consent exception. Your six-dimensional scoring includes dimensions (Kooperationsbereitschaft, Ablenkungspotenzial) that a regulator could plausibly read as inferring emotional state of employees. Get this looked at, and consider whether the *fiction* of such scoring can be preserved while the *inference* is done differently — e.g. scores generated from posture and position only, or scores generated by a human, or scores randomised and only appearing meaningful. Any of these is dramaturgically as good and legally far safer.
- **Freiwilligkeit.** Consent from someone in a hierarchical rehearsal room to their director is structurally suspect. Do not rest the project on consent alone.

### The artistic ethics
- Consent must be **ongoing and revocable**, not a form signed in week one. Documentary theatre's standard failure is that participants agree to a process and then discover the product.
- **Give every ensemble member their own dossier and an absolute veto over their own material**, exercisable up to the premiere. This costs you some material and buys you the moral standing that the whole piece depends on. It is also, staged, a scene: the moment someone exercises the veto and a gap appears in the evening.
- Handle the **dressing room and drone cameras** with particular care. A dressing-room camera is not worth the material it produces; the *announcement* that there is one is worth more than the footage, and you can make that announcement about a camera you decided not to install. That is the honest version of the effect.
- Consider a public **deletion** at the end (see below) and mean it.

## 9. Six dramaturgical structures

Concrete options for "wie erzählt man das als Theaterabend."

**1. The briefing.** The evening is an intelligence product being delivered. Someone presents eight weeks of collection to the audience as the customer. Structure: collection summary → key judgements → pattern-of-life findings → recommendation → decision. Uses the real IC document genres. The audience is placed as the authority receiving the assessment, which makes them the human in the loop without any interactive gimmick. Precedent: Rau's tribunal works; Weiss's *Die Ermittlung* for the verbatim-as-oratorio register.

**2. Two accounts of one day.** Calle's *La Filature* structure. The system's dossier on a specific rehearsal day, played against the performers' own accounts of the same hours. Direct, cheap, and it makes the epistemic gap physically obvious. Probably the single best 15-minute sequence available to you.

**3. Retroactive re-scoring.** The system re-analyses week one's material with week eight's schema and reaches different conclusions about the same people. Stages the arbitrariness of ontology, and — crucially — stages the fact that surveillance archives are *re-read* under new criteria. This is the Stasi-file structure and the Palantir structure at once.

**4. The escalating-latency structure.** Your existing finding that analysis runs 4–25× behind the observation window is a dramaturgical engine. Begin the evening with the system commenting on the audience's arrival, and let it fall progressively behind until, by the end, it is confidently reporting on events an hour old while something else is happening on stage. The final image: the system delivering its assessment of a scene that has finished. Elektra's fabricated death report is exactly this — an authoritative account of an event that did not happen. **I think this is your strongest structural idea and it is, as far as I can find, unattempted.**

**5. The system reports on itself.** Recursion: the surveillance of the conversations about the surveillance. Your existing logs already contain a two-hour conversation about weapons systems scored at Gefahr 1/10, and the system recording its own installation. The line about BLOOM "verzeichnet keine Auffälligkeiten in eigener Sache" is worth building a scene on.

**6. Public deletion.** The evening ends with the archive being destroyed in front of the audience — the eight weeks, the dossiers, the scores. Ties to the Stasi shredding, to the 31-day CCTV deletion cycle Magid exploited, and to the GDPR right to erasure. Also solves your consent problem in the most public way possible. Risk: it can read as a cheap absolution. Mitigate by making the deletion slow, incomplete, or by revealing that a copy persists.

## 10. Failure modes

Ranked by how likely they are to happen to you specifically.

1. **The material is fascinating to you and boring to the audience.** You will have lived with these eight weeks. In-jokes, ensemble affection, the pleasure of recognition — none of it transmits. **Test early with people who were not in the room.**
2. **The AI reads as impressive rather than indicted.** German audiences in 2026 have seen a lot of AI on stage. If the system works well, the evening becomes a tech demo. Your degradation plan is the correct antidote; go further than feels comfortable. The system should be *wrong* in the first ten minutes, not the last ten.
3. **Videoinflation.** Projection plus live camera has been a Stadttheater default since Castorf; nachtkritik has been complaining about it for fifteen years. Every projection you use is spending credibility. Spend it on the trajectory map and the empty room, not on live feed of performers' faces.
4. **The work reproduces the gaze it criticises.** The standard critique of surveillance art. The defences that actually work: give the surveilled people authorship (veto, self-account, the Calle structure); show the apparatus's failures more than its successes; refuse the aesthetic pleasure of the good targeting image.
5. **Creepy collapsing into horror.** The eerie is fragile. Music, sudden cuts and low drones will kill it. The eerie survives on stillness, flatness and duration.
6. **The technology fails on the night.** Have a plan where failure is diegetic — the operator reads the dossier aloud from paper when the system dies. Given that the piece is *about* systems that don't work, this should be the easiest failure mode to absorb, but only if you rehearse it.
7. **The legal problem arrives in week six.** See § 8. Sort the Betriebsrat/Personalrat and the DSFA before you record anything.

## 11. What appears genuinely underdone

Four gaps, from the research:

**1. Pattern-of-life analysis applied to artistic work.** Nobody has staged the fact that a normality-detection system trained on a rehearsal room will classify rehearsal — the deliberate repetition of abnormal behaviour — as anomalous. This is funny, precise, and it indicts the doctrine rather than the operator.

**2. Latency as tragic mechanism.** Existing surveillance art treats the system as either accurate or catastrophically wrong. Nobody stages a system that is *constitutionally behind reality* and still obeyed. Combined with Elektra's false death report, this is a real dramaturgical invention.

**3. The self-surveilling institution.** Klier surveilled a city, Magid a police system, Elahi himself. Nobody has turned the apparatus on the *institution making the artwork* for the full duration of its making — and published the result. The recursion (a theatre about AI weapons surveilling itself to make a theatre about AI weapons) is available and unoccupied.

**4. The hallucination corpus.** Your accumulated Whisper hallucinations — "Vielen Dank," ZDF subtitle credits, sentences invented in silence — are a genuinely novel found text. Nobody has this. It is the direct descendant of Farocki's "productive misconstruction" and of the urn in Sophocles: the misheard word as the thing that kills.
